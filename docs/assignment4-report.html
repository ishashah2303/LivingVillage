2. MVP 2.0: the full loop

Start state: a menu over the live village picks the mode (Fire Season challenge or Sandbox) and the planner's LLM backbone (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 or rules only). Active state: in Fire Season the player has 4 minutes until the rains come. A director starts fires faster and faster, unattended fires spread, smoke hurts villagers, and a food shortage hits midway. The player has water buckets (refilling), food baskets and an alarm bell; the villagers' behavior trees, the Detour crowd, the boids and the LLM planner do the rest. The HUD shows time left, how much of the village stands, who is hurt and what is burning. End state: the village wins when the rains come (1–3 stars and a score) and loses at 0% integrity or 3 villagers hurt. Both lead to a results screen (time held out, fires put out, planner commands accepted / rejected, mean planner latency, LLM cost), then Play again or Main menu. Sandbox keeps the Assignment 3 world editor and ends with a session summary. All pillars run inside the game: the behavior tree got a new branch (a hurt guard stops fighting and flees), navmesh obstacles change with every fire, the flock scatters from fires, and the planner coordinates evacuations.

3. Release notes (since Assignment 3)

New features

  • Game loop: start menu, Fire Season challenge, win / loss / session end screens, Play again and Main menu (each game gets a fresh simulation).
  • HUD (timer, village integrity, hurt, burning, pause banner); player tools with charges and cooldown; keys 1–3, B, Space.
  • Game director with escalating waves; fire spread; health, smoke damage and starvation; injured villagers leave the map.
  • Planner backbone chosen per game in the menu; every plan reports its tokens and list-price cost (planner panel and end screen).
  • Backbone benchmark (server/bench) over scenarios exported from real simulation runs.
  • Tests: 31 simulation tests (+11, including full games played headless: an idle player must lose, a simple bot must win) and 18 server tests (+2).

Bug fixes

  • Haiku plans were cut off at max_tokens 4096 on large crowds and came back empty; raised to 16k (found by the benchmark).
  • Server grounding did not enforce the 3-villagers-per-spot limit; it now drops the extra command.
  • A planner reply arriving after a restart could touch a freed navmesh; the WASM navmesh is now disposed on restart and late replies are ignored.
  • The "Stale plan" demo left the simulated latency at 4 s for the rest of the session.
  • The top bar overflowed at phone width.

Known issues

  • LLM latency has long tails (one Opus call took 92 s, one Haiku call 64 s); after 30 s the rules answer instead, so the village never waits, but that plan is lost.
  • A Fire Season game sends about 15–20 plans; the hosted planner's spend cap (20 Claude plans per visitor per hour) makes a second game in the same hour fall back to the rules.
  • Evacuation coverage drops to ~65% at 32 villagers for every planner: 11 safe spots × capacity 3 is not enough (Week 10).
  • Villagers asleep indoors ignore a fire next to their house.
  • Balance is tuned with headless bot games only; no human playtest yet (Week 12).
  • Characters are still procedural, there is no audio, and the free Render tier sleeps after 15 idle minutes.

4. Response to feedback: other LLM backbones and scaling

Same prompt, schema and grounding for every model, effort low, 7 scenarios exported from real runs (5 with 8 villagers, 2 synthetic crowds of 24 and 32), 2 runs each; 56 calls cost about $0.82 in total. Coverage = share of villagers within 14 m of a fire given a valid evacuation; validity = share of the model's commands that pass grounding.

* about 17 plans in an 8-villager Fire Season game. Prices: Opus $4/$20, Sonnet $2/$10, Haiku $0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens.

  • Backbones. On our task Sonnet 5.5 is the best default: fastest (4.2 s median), half of Opus's cost, similar coverage; its 3% invalid commands (spots too close to a fire) are all caught by grounding. Haiku is ~20× cheaper than Opus with equal coverage but thinks long even at low effort (2.5k output tokens on average), so it is not faster. The menu now defaults to Sonnet, and Week 12 adds a Haiku-first cascade.
  • Scaling. The prompt grows linearly with the cast (2.9k → 8.9k input tokens from 8 to 32 villagers; Sonnet and Opus latency ×2–2.6), and coverage is capped by spot capacity, not by the model. Planned algorithms: min-cost flow for capacity-constrained assignment and flow fields for mass evacuation (W10); hierarchical planning so the LLM plans per group (W12); BT time-slicing, spatial hashing and instancing for characters and objects (W10); one hazard abstraction with per-hazard navmesh area costs for new emergency types (W11).
